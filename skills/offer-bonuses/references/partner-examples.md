# Partner bonuses and commissions

## Local pain clinic with a $400 offer

The clinic approaches non-competing local businesses its patients will likely use next. Each partner gives the clinic's buyers something free or discounted and, in return, reaches qualified prospects it did not pay to find.

| Partner business | What the buyer receives | Stated value | What the partner pays the clinic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chiropractor | Two free adjustments | $100 | $100 per referred visit |
| Orthotics supplier | Product discount | $150 saved | $100 per referred person |
| Pharmacy | Prescription discount | $100 saved per month | $100 per referred person |
| Gym | One training session and one month of pool access | $100 | Free membership, or $50 per person who joins |
| Low-inflammation food company | Product discount | $50 saved | Free food |
| Massage therapist | One or two free massages | none given | none given |

Several providers of the same service can be stacked, for example ten chiropractors each giving one free adjustment. The partner bonuses alone are worth more than the $400 price, and the commissions add about $350 of profit per sale, paid by partners for customers the clinic already spent money to acquire.

## Group discount plus commission

A supplement company sells through gym owners. Gym members buy at 30 percent below the public price, and the gym owner earns 40 percent of each sale after the discount. Members pay less, the gym owner is paid for offering an exclusive discount, and the supplement company gains customers for the cost of the commission.

## Examples for small software and service products

A small paid SaaS tool for freelancers can partner with a non-competing invoicing tool and a bookkeeping service. The invoicing tool gives buyers three free months, the bookkeeping service gives a discounted first month, and each pays the user a fixed fee per referred signup. The stated value of each partner bonus is the price the buyer would otherwise pay.
