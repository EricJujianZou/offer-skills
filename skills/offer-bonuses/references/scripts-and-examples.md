# Scripts and examples for bonuses

The scripts are grouped by the situation the user is in. Adapt every script to the user's product.

## Find the section by situation

| The user's situation | Section to use |
|---|---|
| A long list of deliverables, unsure which become bonuses | Core or bonus: the wow test |
| A prospect said no or asked for a discount in a call or chat | Prospect says no: one-on-one sequence |
| A bonus has a real limit or a real deadline | Scarcity or urgency on one bonus |
| No bonuses ready for the next offer | Vault assets by use |
| Small software tool, cohort course or freelance service | Examples for small software and service products |

## Core or bonus: the wow test

An item becomes a bonus when it would make the buyer say "wow" and the seller would hate for the buyer to miss it. Distinct, short, high-value items fit best, such as checklists, launch maps and template packs, because they pack a lot into a small space and could almost be sold on their own. Anything the buyer must use to get the result stays in the core.

## Prospect says no: one-on-one sequence

1. The seller asks for the sale and mentions no bonus yet.
2. A buyer who says yes hears the bonuses after signup, which reinforces the decision.
3. A buyer who says no hears the seller agree with the concern, add the one bonus that answers it, and ask again, ending with "fair enough?".
4. The seller repeats step 3 with the next bonus for the next concern. Each added bonus makes a refusal harder.

The core price stays fixed throughout, because a discount teaches buyers that prices move when they push. Every item shown was going to be delivered anyway, so the sequence raises perceived value without adding cost. Television sales used the same method for decades, adding items to a fixed price until refusing felt unreasonable, and paid airtime would have killed it if it did not return its cost.

## Scarcity or urgency on one bonus

| Type | Use it when | Script pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Scarcity, exclusive access | The bonus is not sold or offered anywhere else | `Bonuses 1, 2 and 3 are only available to people who join [program]. I don't sell them separately and you won't find them anywhere else.` |
| Scarcity, limited count | A fixed number of seats or units exists, for example 3 seats at a $5,000 event | `There are [3] seats left at my [$5,000] [event]. If you join [program], one of them is yours as a bonus.` |
| Urgency, today only | The bonus is withdrawn after a real deadline | `If you join today, I'll include [bonus], which normally costs [$1,000], at no charge. I do this to reward people who make a decision.` |

Scarcity scripts have no date and say the bonus cannot be had any other way. The urgency script has a date, and the buyer loses the bonus by waiting.

## Vault assets by use

| Use | Asset to build |
|---|---|
| Answer a "no time" or "too much work" objection | Checklists, templates, scripts, swipe files: made once, given away without limit |
| Answer a specific doubt when it comes up | Recordings of every workshop, webinar, event and interview |
| Solve the buyer's next problem | Group discounts and referral commissions with adjacent, non-competing businesses |
| Raise value at almost no cost | Information products behind a login, tickets to virtual events |
| Bonus that also sells an upgrade | One month of a higher service tier at a fixed cost |

## Examples for small software and service products

Paid Chrome extension at $5 a month. The prospect says it is too expensive. The seller keeps the price, agrees that cost matters, and adds a bonus that answers the stated worry, for example a "Best Settings in 5 Minutes" checklist with a stated value equal to a 30 minute setup call. The seller then asks "fair enough?".

Cohort course. Every live session is recorded and stored as a vault. A prospect who worries about falling behind is offered the recorded catch-up session as a bonus with a stated value. Buyers who will need hosting or a domain next get a negotiated discount from a non-competing tool, and the user earns a referral commission.

Freelance service. A prospect worries about the handover after the project ends. The seller adds a maintenance checklist and a recorded walkthrough of the finished work, each with a stated value, and keeps the project fee unchanged.
