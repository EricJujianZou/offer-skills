# Guarantee catalogue

Every guarantee follows one template: if the buyer does not get X in Y time, the seller will do Z. Pick the kind first with the table in SKILL.md, then use the two tables below to find the named guarantees that fit, and read their entries.

## Find candidates by buyer objection

| The buyer's main objection | Named guarantees that answer it |
|---|---|
| "It might not work for me" | No Questions Asked Refund, Outsized Refund, Credit-based |
| "I might not do the work" | Service, Modified Service, Personal Service, All Sales Are Final (the self-starter form) |
| "I cannot afford the risk up front" | Delayed Second Payment, First Outcome, Hotel + Airfare Perks, Implied (performance or revenue share) |
| "The seller might not deliver" | Satisfaction-Based Refund, Wage-Payment, Release of Service |

## Find candidates by product type

| Product type | Usually fits | Usually avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Digital product (extension, template, small SaaS tool, low price) | No Questions Asked Refund, Outsized Refund on a high-margin product, All Sales Are Final when it is easy to copy once seen, Credit-based on an upsell | Service and Personal Service, since there is no ongoing delivery to extend |
| Service (freelance, agency, done-for-you, paid calls) | Service, Delayed Second Payment, First Outcome, Wage-Payment for single calls, Release of Service for contracts, Implied when results are measurable | Unconditional refunds when delivery cost is high |
| High-ticket (coaching, consulting, high-price custom work) | Outsized Refund with strict conditions, Personal Service, All Sales Are Final (self-starter form), Implied with a minimum fee | Satisfaction-Based Refund, since a refund after costly delivery loses both |
| Cohort (course with a start date, workshop, live event) | Modified Service (free place in later cohorts), Service, Delayed Second Payment, Hotel + Airfare Perks for in-person events, a short-window unconditional refund stacked with a conditional promise | Open-ended Personal Service, since one instructor's time does not scale across a cohort |

## Objection: "It might not work for me"

**No Questions Asked Refund** (unconditional)
- What it is: money back for any reason, in one of five variants: full refund, 50 percent back, the buyer's ad spend and other outside costs back, the seller pays for a competitor's program, or the price back plus a fixed extra sum such as $1,000.
- Condition: none from the buyer. The seller must deliver well and must have run the refund math. Each condition added to it makes it weaker.
- Risk and cost: pays out on buyer failures as well as seller failures, and it is common, so it does little to set an offer apart. Cost is the price plus any delivery already spent on each claimant.

**Outsized Refund** (conditional)
- What it is: double or triple the money back, or a fixed payment far larger than the price.
- Condition: high margin, plus a list of actions the buyer must complete to qualify, chosen so that buyers who complete them almost always succeed.
- Risk and cost: large payouts when conditions are loose. In the book's case, a $2,997 course offered to buy a failed buyer's store for $25,000; ten claims ($250,000) were paid against about $3M in added sales. This type usually beats a standard 30-day refund on net conversions (sales minus refunds).

**Credit-based** (conditional)
- What it is: the amount paid comes back as credit toward any other product the seller offers.
- Condition: an existing customer who already likes one of the seller's products, unsure about a new one during an upsell.
- Risk and cost: low. No cash leaves the business, and the worst case is the customer spending the credit on a product they already value.

## Objection: "I might not do the work"

**Service** (conditional)
- What it is: the seller keeps working for free until X is achieved, with no deadline, so money already collected is never handed back.
- Condition: optional but recommended. The buyer completes the actions linked to success, for example setting up a page, attending calls or reporting data.
- Risk and cost: unpaid delivery for buyers who miss the result, limited by the action conditions. Claims are rare in practice, because a buyer who did every action is usually close to the result and satisfied by then.

**Modified Service** (conditional)
- What it is: another period of service or access for free, at least twice as long as the original.
- Condition: the same action conditions as Service, with a cap on the extra time. It is an easier first step before full Service.
- Risk and cost: bounded to the delivery cost of the extra period.

**Personal Service** (conditional)
- What it is: the owner works one-on-one with the buyer, free, until the result. It grows stronger as the owner becomes better known.
- Condition: strict, for example replying within 24 hours, using the tools the seller specifies, or having already spent a set amount (the book uses $10,000) running the seller's method.
- Risk and cost: the owner's time. Choose conditions so that anyone who meets them almost always succeeds; without conditions it sells well and cannot be fulfilled.

**All Sales Are Final** (anti-guarantee)
- What it is: no refund, stated firmly with a true reason. It works as an admission against interest, since the seller admits buyers could take the product and leave. Three reason forms: once used it cannot be unused; the buyer will see the seller's own live process (funnels, ads, metrics), so the business is exposed; or the work is high-price and effortful and the offer is only for self-starters who follow instructions.
- Condition: a product that is consumable, loses most of its value once delivered or is easy to copy once seen (a checkout code snippet, a set of opening messages, cold outreach lines), or high-price heavy-effort work. The reason must be true.
- Risk and cost: fewer sales to buyers who need reassurance; the book gives no figure. No refunds are paid.

## Objection: "I cannot afford the risk up front"

**Delayed Second Payment** (conditional)
- What it is: no second charge until the buyer reaches a first outcome, such as a first sale, a live website or the first five pounds lost.
- Condition: a systematized path to the first result and a known activation metric (the early action that predicts long-term retention).
- Risk and cost: cash flow waits on the buyer's first result. It pushes the buyer to act fast and points the seller's team at activating each buyer.

**First Outcome** (conditional)
- What it is: the seller covers the buyer's outside costs (ad spend, travel) until the first outcome. The book's case covers ad spend from day 14 until the first sale if none came in the first 14 days.
- Condition: a measurable first outcome and an outside cost the seller can afford to cover.
- Risk and cost: open-ended outside spending until the first result. It keeps both sides focused on the first dollar, since the second tends to follow soon.

**Hotel + Airfare Perks** (conditional)
- What it is: if the event gives no value, the seller refunds the ticket plus the buyer's hotel and airfare.
- Condition: an in-person product such as a workshop or live event.
- Risk and cost: the payout is larger than the ticket price. It is a variant of the outside-cost refund, similar to adding about $1,000 to the guarantee but more concrete and easier to remember.

**Implied (performance or revenue share)** (implied)
- What it is: the buyer pays only when the seller performs, on terms agreed before work starts. Forms: a fee per sale, booked appointment or unit of result; revenue share (for example 10 percent of top line, or 25 percent of growth over a baseline); profit share (a percentage of profit or gross profit, for example 20 percent); ratchets (10 percent above X, 20 percent above Y, 30 percent above Z); or a set payment when a named event occurs.
- Condition: quantifiable outcomes, open measurement, and trust or control that payment can be collected.
- Risk and cost: unpaid work if results do not come, plus the work of tracking and collecting. The book ranks it among the most desirable setups because it ties the seller to the buyer's results. See the minimums below.

## Objection: "The seller might not deliver"

**Satisfaction-Based Refund** (unconditional)
- What it is: a refund at any time if the buyer is not satisfied with the level of service. The promise covers the service, for example a stated amount of value and support, not the result the buyer controls.
- Condition: the seller must deliver that service well. It suits lower prices and becomes risky at high prices with high delivery cost.
- Risk and cost: the strongest form, since the seller can do everything right and still owe a refund. The book's case saw 2 claims from 4,000 buyers over three and a half years.

**Wage-Payment** (conditional)
- What it is: if a call or session was not worth the buyer's time, the seller pays the buyer an hourly rate for it.
- Condition: a call or session format and a rate set in advance. The book derives the rate from annual income divided by 1,960 working hours (40 hours for 49 weeks) and asks for a tax return; this skill does not use that claim process. Publish a fixed rate or use a rate the buyer states.
- Risk and cost: low, since the payout is capped at one session.

**Release of Service** (conditional)
- What it is: release from the contract at no charge, which cancels any commitment or cancellation fee.
- Condition: a business with contracts or fees it can enforce. A business that does not enforce them gives up nothing by offering it.
- Risk and cost: the remaining contract value from buyers who leave.

## Performance pricing minimums

Performance pay can carry a floor:
- The greater of a fixed fee (for example $1,000) or a percentage of revenue generated (for example 10 percent). This covers delivery cost if the buyer generates nothing.
- A fixed monthly fee for the first few months (for example $1,000 a month for 3 months), then pure performance pay. This suits work that is slow to produce results.

The version with no guaranteed payment is the stronger offer.

## Stacking

Guarantees can be combined:
- Unconditional plus conditional, for example a 30-day no-questions refund plus a 90-day conditional triple-your-money-back promise.
- Two conditional promises on sequential outcomes, for example a first revenue target by day 60 and a larger one by day 90, each tied to stated actions. The buyer pictures reaching each milestone, which makes the outcome feel more likely.

## Conditions as a way to get the buyer to act

When the seller knows the actions behind success, those actions should be the conditions. More buyers then succeed, so fewer claim. Most buyers who can choose between a refund and the promised outcome choose the outcome.

## Refund math example

- Without a guarantee: 100 sales, 5 refunds (5 percent), 95 net sales.
- With a guarantee: 130 sales, 13 refunds (10 percent), 117 net sales.
- 117 divided by 95 is 1.23, so net sales rose 23 percent even though the refund rate doubled.

Net multiple = (new sales × (1 − new refund rate)) / (old sales × (1 − old refund rate)). Use 0 for the old refund rate if no guarantee existed. A result above 1.0 means the guarantee raised net sales. For high-cost delivery, also subtract the delivery cost already spent on each refunded buyer, which is why those businesses lean toward conditional guarantees and anti-guarantees.

## Naming and stating the guarantee

- Give it a specific name. A plain "30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee" is the weak form. A stronger form describes how much the buyer would want to keep the product, for example a refund of every dollar if after 30 days the buyer would not go out of their way to get it back.
- State the claim process in the copy: any reason qualifies (for an unconditional refund), one action to claim, such as an email with one word to a named address, and a concrete response time.
- A useful close sets out the best case (the buyer gets the result) and the worst case (the buyer gets the money back and kept the service received), so the only choice that cannot help is not buying.
- Naming the offer as a whole belongs to the offer-naming skill (it may be namespaced in some installs, for example offer-skills:offer-naming).

## Examples for small software and service products

| Product | Named guarantee | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| $9 Chrome extension for students | No Questions Asked Refund | "Use it for a full week of classes. If it did not save you time, email refund@ with the word 'refund' and the money is back the same day." |
| $49 cold email template pack | Outsized Refund | "Send 100 emails with these templates on the schedule in the guide. If you book zero calls, I pay you $150." |
| 4-week cohort course | Modified Service | "Attend every session and submit every weekly check-in. If your product page is not live with a working signup form by the end of week 4, you join the next two cohorts free." |
| Paid 45-minute portfolio review | Wage-Payment | "If the call was not worth your time, I pay you $60 for the 45 minutes." |
| Two-payment Shopify store setup service | Delayed Second Payment | "The second payment is not charged until your store takes its first order." |
| Template pack of cold outreach scripts and a tracking spreadsheet | All Sales Are Final | "You get every script and the full tracker. You can copy all of it in ten minutes, so all sales are final." |
| Landing page for a local tutoring business | Implied (performance or revenue share) | "$0 upfront, $15 per booked trial lesson that comes through the page, counted by the booking form." |
