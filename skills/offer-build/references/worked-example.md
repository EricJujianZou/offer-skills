# Worked examples

## The book's gym offer, in summary

- The offer: a gym's weight loss program with the dream outcome "Lose 20lbs in 6 weeks", replacing a $99 per month bootcamp that lost on price to a $29 per month chain gym.
- Step 3 produced 16 core problems with two to four sub-problems each, so 32 to 64 problems in total.
- The final stack had seven bundles: a grocery system, a five-minute cooking guide, a personal meal plan, fat loss workouts, a travel eating and workout plan, an accountability system and an eating-out system.
- Its main low-tier asset was a meal plan spreadsheet that took about 100 hours to build and then produced each personalised plan in about 15 minutes.
- The stated value was $4,351 and the price was $599, about 14 percent. The seven listed values sum to $4,347; the offer skills use the stated $4,351.

## Examples for small software and service products

### A cohort course on search ads for solo software founders

The product is a six-week live cohort course that teaches solo founders of small paid software tools to run search ads for their own product. It runs in groups of up to 24 buyers. Before this procedure, the user described it as "a six-week course with weekly calls" and priced it against free video tutorials, which buyers chose instead.

The steps below carry the step numbers used in SKILL.md.

#### Step 2: Dream outcome

"Get your first 10 paying customers from search ads in 6 weeks, spending under $1,000 on ads."

The 10 customers are the result. The 6 weeks shortens the time delay, and the spending cap answers the buyer's fear of losing money before the ads work.

#### Step 3: Problems in the order the buyer meets them

The buyer's activities, in order: setting up the ad account and conversion tracking, choosing keywords, writing the ads and the landing page, launching and setting a budget, reading results and adjusting, and keeping the ads running after the course ends. The last activity is the step after the course, which buyers often leave out.

| Activity | Not worth the money | Will not work for me, or I will not stick with it | Too hard or unpleasant | Takes too long |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Account and tracking setup | Ad spend is wasted if tracking is wrong | My product's checkout is unusual and the guides do not cover it | Tracking setup is technical and confusing | Setup eats a week of evenings |
| Choosing keywords | I will pay for clicks from people who never buy | My niche has no search volume | Keyword tools show too many numbers | Research takes days |
| Writing ads and the landing page | My copy will not convert, so spend is wasted | I am not a writer | Writing about my own product feels awkward | I keep rewriting and never ship |
| Launch and budget | I might overspend in the first week | I will panic and switch the ads off too early | Bids and budgets are confusing | Waiting for data feels endless |
| Reading results and adjusting | I cannot tell if the ads pay for themselves | I will fall behind the cohort and drop out | The reports are hard to read | Weekly adjustments do not fit my schedule |
| After the course | The results stop once I stop paying attention | I will forget the routine | I will not know what to change when results drop | I cannot spend hours a week on ads forever |

That gives 24 core problems. Most have one or two sub-problems (for example "my checkout is unusual" splits into "I use a third-party checkout" and "my product has a free trial before payment"), so the full list is about 40.

#### Step 4: Problems turned into "how to" solutions

Account and tracking setup:

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Ad spend wasted by broken tracking | How to confirm tracking works before spending the first dollar |
| Unusual checkout | How to track a sale through a third-party checkout or a free trial |
| Technical and confusing | How to set up tracking by following a checklist, with no code |
| Takes a week of evenings | How to finish setup in one 90-minute sitting |

Reading results and adjusting:

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Cannot tell if the ads pay for themselves | How to read cost per paying customer from one number |
| Falling behind the cohort | How to catch up in 30 minutes after missing a week |
| Reports are hard to read | How to check the three figures that matter and ignore the rest |
| Adjustments do not fit the schedule | How to make the weekly adjustment in 20 minutes |

The other activities get solutions the same way, such as "how to find keywords people type when they are ready to buy" and "how to write an ad from a template in 15 minutes". Several solutions repeat across activities, which is expected.

#### Step 5: Delivery options for one problem

Problem: tracking setup is technical and confusing.

| Attention | Option | Cost tier |
|---|---|---|
| One-to-one | Done-for-you tracking setup in the buyer's account | high |
| One-to-one | Screen-share setup call | high |
| One-to-one | Written review of the buyer's setup within one business day | high |
| Small group | Live 90-minute setup workshop for 8 buyers, everyone setting up at once | medium |
| Small group | Setup pairs: two buyers check each other's tracking | low |
| One-to-many | Recorded setup walkthrough per common checkout provider | low |
| One-to-many | Printable setup checklist | low |
| One-to-many | A tracking test page that shows a green tick when a test sale is recorded | low |

#### Steps 6 and 7: Trim, stack and value

Trim decisions:

- Done-for-you tracking setup: high cost, and the buyer learns nothing, so the problem returns after the course. Stepped back to the live group setup workshop.
- Weekly one-to-one calls: high cost and high value. Stepped back to one 20-minute one-to-one account review in week 3, when most buyers have their first data.
- Chat support around the clock: high cost and low extra value over a next-business-day reply. Stepped back to cohort chat answered once a day.
- A printed workbook: low value. Cut.
- The tracking test page, checklists and templates: low cost and high value. Kept as the base of the stack.

| Item | What problem it solves | Delivery method | Cost tier | Stated value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tracking setup kit | Tracking is confusing, and broken tracking wastes ad spend | one-to-many recordings, checklist and test page, plus one small group live workshop on video call | medium | $400 |
| Buyer keyword finder | Paying for clicks from people who never buy | one-to-many spreadsheet template | low | $300 |
| Ad and page copy templates with a group review | Copy that does not convert, and not being a writer | one-to-many templates, plus one small group live review on video call | medium | $500 |
| Budget guardrail calculator | Overspending in the first week | one-to-many calculator | low | $150 |
| Weekly group clinic | Not knowing what to adjust, and dropping out | small group, six 90-minute video calls | medium | $600 |
| Week 3 account review | Not knowing whether the ads pay for themselves | one-to-one, one 20-minute video call | high | $250 |
| Cohort chat | Getting stuck between sessions | one-to-many chat, answered once a day | medium | $300 |
| 20-minute monthly routine | Results dropping after the course ends | one-to-many recording and checklist | low | $200 |

Total stated value: $2,700.

Basis for the stated values:

- Tracking setup kit: a freelance specialist typically charges about $400 to set up conversion tracking.
- Buyer keyword finder: it saves about 6 hours of research at $50 per hour, and the negative keyword list it produces avoids wasted first-month spend.
- Copy templates and review: close to what a freelance copywriter charges for one ad set and a landing page edit.
- Budget guardrail calculator: it caps the first week's spend, which protects the buyer from overspending by about $150 in a typical first-week mistake.
- Weekly group clinic: six sessions at $100 each, below what a one-to-one consultant charges per hour.
- Week 3 account review: the price of a short paid account audit from a consultant.
- Cohort chat and monthly routine: the hours they save the buyer at $50 per hour over the six weeks and the first months after.

Proposed price: $497, about 18 percent of the stated value, with the user told to run the offer-market skill to confirm it. The only high-tier item is 20 minutes of one-to-one time per buyer, and the medium-tier items are shared across the cohort of 24, so the margin stays wide.

Fast win: tracking confirmed by the test page and the first ads approved within 7 days of purchase, before the buyer has spent anything on clicks.

Problems left unsolved: "my niche has no search volume". The keyword finder shows this in week 1, and the user decides whether to refund those buyers or point them to another channel. offer-guarantees can turn that decision into a condition.
