# Scarcity examples and scripts

## Pick the row that matches the user's situation

Rows are grouped by product type: software, service, group, then physical. Cohort start dates and other dates go to offer-urgency.

| Situation | Form | The limit | Page copy | Sales line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Software with no delivery limit | Founding batch | A fixed number of early buyers whose price or terms will not be offered again, used only if the seller will not reopen it | "The founding price is open to the first 50 buyers and will not return." | "You would be one of the 50 founding buyers, and that price closes when the batch fills." |
| Free lead magnet that gets too little interest | Weekly download cap | Downloads per week the seller chooses; people who wait tend to use it more | "This week's 20 copies are gone. Leave your email and we will tell you when the next 20 open." | Not used on calls. |
| Highest service tier, ongoing | Total business cap | Clients the business can serve at this level at once; raise by 10 to 20 percent from time to time, then cap again | "We work with 25 clients in total and we are full. Join the waiting list and we will contact you when a spot opens." | "We are full right now. I can put you first on the list for the next opening." |
| Service with a known weekly onboarding capacity | Growth rate cap | New clients the seller can onboard per week | "We start 5 new clients a week. 3 of this week's spots are taken." | "Two spots are left this week and I have six more calls booked. You can take one now or wait for next week." |
| Higher-priced upsell: workshop, event or consulting block | Workshop or event seats | Seats the seller's time allows; open fewer than expected demand | "20 seats for this workshop." | "I am opening 20 seats and expect them to go in the first week." |
| Seller with an audience who will answer direct messages or calls | One-to-one access tier | A tiny number of high-priced spots for messages, email, phone or video calls, through a channel the seller does not mind using | "3 spots for direct access this quarter." | "I keep only three direct-access spots, and one is open." |
| Any capped service, stating how full it is | Fill level statement on top of another cap | Clients the seller can serve this period, with the point where "full" sits chosen by the seller | "We are at 81 percent of capacity this month." | "We are mostly booked this month, which is why I would decide this week." |
| People start together in a group | Cohort cap | People one cohort can serve, for example the feedback the instructor can give | "Each cohort takes 100 people. Enrolment closes when it is full." | "This cohort has 14 seats left. Once it fills, the next chance is the following cohort." |
| Small group such as a mastermind | Members who leave cannot return | Membership size of the group; weakens as the group grows | "Membership is capped. A member who leaves gives up the spot for good." | "If you leave, the spot goes to the waiting list and does not come back." |
| Physical or digital product made in batches | Limited release | A fixed batch, about once a month and no more often, sized to sell out every time; announce each sell-out so buyers who waited act sooner next time | "This month we are making 100 boxes of the mint protein bar. When they are gone, this flavour is gone." | "This batch is 100 boxes and most are spoken for." |
| One-off item or edition | Never available again | A fixed run (for example 100 hoodies) that the seller states will not be made again | "100 hoodies in this colourway. We will not make it again." | "Once these 100 are gone, this design is retired." |

A stated fill level also works as social proof, since it shows other people already bought.

## Why a rare seller can charge more

Buyers assume a recognised expert has heavy demand for a small supply of time, so they accept a higher rate. A buyer with a narrow, pressing problem also sees very few people who can solve it. Value comes from how rare the source is and how large the delivered result is, so a high day rate is a good deal when the result is many times the fee.

## Examples for small software and service products

- **Cohort course.** The instructor can give feedback to 12 projects a week, so each cohort is capped at 12. Page copy: "This cohort takes 12 people. 9 spots are taken." The sold-out page collects emails for the next cohort. Condition: 12 is the real feedback limit and more than 12 people are expected to apply.
- **Paid Chrome extension or small SaaS tool.** The software has no delivery limit, so a cap on licences would be invented. Honest options are a one-to-one setup tier capped at the calls the seller will take per week (growth rate cap), or a founding batch of fixed size whose price or terms will not be offered again (never available again), used only if the seller will not reopen it.
- **Freelance service.** A freelancer who can run 3 projects at once states that 3 project slots exist and names how many are open, with a waiting list for the rest (total business cap).
- **Landing page waitlist.** A beta admits a fixed number of waitlist members per week, sized to what the seller can support. This follows the weekly download cap row. The day each group opens is urgency and goes to offer-urgency.
- **Local service business.** A cleaning or repair business that can book 10 new recurring customers a month says so and lists the remaining openings (growth rate cap per month).
