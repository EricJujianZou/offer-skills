# Offer naming: word lists and worked examples

## Word lists for each MAGIC part

The book maps the five parts to marketing ideas: Magnet to attention, Avatar to discrimination (who it is for and who it is not for), Goal to purpose, Interval to timeline, Container to method.

**Magnet (the reason why).** Answers why the offer is being made, why the buyer should respond, or both.
Words by offer type:
- Any offer: Free, Percent Off, Giveaway, Anniversary, Thank You, Members Only.
- Course or cohort: Founding Cohort, Early Bird Enrollment, New Year, Back To School, Summer, Spring.
- Software tool: Early Access, Beta, Launch Week, Version 2, Founding Member Pricing, Open Source Release.
- Service: Tax Season, Year End, New Management, Grand Opening, New Building, First Client Discount.
- Event: Black Friday, Holiday, Halloween, Summer Kickoff, Last Call, Opening Night.

**Avatar.** Specific, and below city level for a local business (a neighbourhood or suburb).
Examples: Etsy Candle Sellers, Northside Dog Owners, Solo Accountants, Shopify Store Owners, Night Shift Nurses, First Time Landlords.

**Goal.** An event, feeling, experience or outcome, as specific and tangible as possible.
Examples: Inbox Zero, First Paying Customer, Clean Books, Sleep Through the Night, Booked Calendar, Zero Late Invoices, First 100 Users.

**Interval.** Minutes, hours, days, weeks or months: "10 Minute", "48 Hour", "14 Day", "6 Week", "3 Month".
Ad platform rule: most ad platforms reject a quantified claim (income, weight, follower count) paired with a duration, because the pair implies a guarantee. "Double Your Revenue in 30 Days" is a claim to avoid on ad platforms. "Launch Your Store in 30 Days" is not a quantified claim, so the interval is fine. Outside ad review, use the interval freely, since duration is one of the strongest parts.

**Container.** Marks a bundle or system that cannot be compared with a commodity alternative.
Words by offer type:
- Course or cohort: Cohort, Bootcamp, Curriculum, Masterclass, Accelerator, Intensive, Mastermind, Challenge, Incubator, Program, Blueprint, Study Plan.
- Software tool: Autopilot, Toolkit, Engine, System, Kit, Tracker, Shortcut, Finder, Cheatcode, Playbook, Hack, Dashboard.
- Service: Audit, Sprint, Tune-Up, Reset, Overhaul, Deep Dive, Detox, Game Plan, Fast Track, Comeback, Solution, Transformation.
- Event or launch: Demo Day, Summit, Retreat, Workshop, Open House, Meetup, Getaway, Experience, Liftoff, Launch, Catapult, Slingshot.

## Rhyme and alliteration

Both are optional. Do not force them. A rhyming dictionary search is a fast way to find a rhyme.

- Rhyme: Tax Prep Step-by-Step, Cash Flow Glow-Up, Lead Feed Speed-Up, Code Review Breakthrough. A rhyme that needs filler words to work is forced and should be dropped.
- Alliteration: Pricing Page Playbook, Support Inbox Sprint, Bookkeeping Bootcamp, Lead List Liftoff, Client Calendar Cleanup.

## Renames and fatigue examples

The book's change order is: creative, body copy, headline, duration, free or discount component, monetization structure. Renames happen at the headline step. Examples:

- Goal word swap: "Free 6 Week Inbox Cleanup Challenge" to "Free 6 Week Inbox Calm Challenge". The deliverable is the same.
- Seasonal reason why: "Summer Store Refresh" to "Black Friday Store Prep". Same service, new season.
- Duration change (step 4, after renames stop helping): "6 Week Bookkeeping Bootcamp" to "28 Day Bookkeeping Bootcamp".
- Reach arithmetic for local fatigue: about $20 reaches 1,000 people, so a 200,000 person area costs about $4,000 to reach once (the book prints a different figure). Reaching everyone once still does not mean the offer is fatigued.

## Examples for small software and service products

**Chrome extension: shows shipping cost next to each product on a shopping site. Free.**
Inputs: buyer is people who shop across several online stores; outcome is seeing the full price before checkout; reason why is the holiday shopping season.
- "Free True Price Finder" (Magnet, Goal, Container, with "Finder" as a software container)
- "Holiday Shopping Total Cost Shortcut" (Magnet, Goal, Container)
- "See Full Checkout Price in 1 Click" (Goal, Interval)
A store listing title has a length limit, which favours the three-part names.

**Landing page waitlist: a scheduling tool for dog groomers, before launch.**
Inputs: buyer is independent dog groomers; outcome is a full calendar with no double bookings; reason why is early access.
- "Early Access: Groomer Calendar Reset" (Magnet, Avatar, Container)
- "Zero Double Bookings This Month: Free Beta for Groomers" (Goal, Interval, Magnet, Avatar)
- "Free 2 Week Booking Bootcamp for Groomers" (Magnet, Interval, Goal, Container, Avatar, with alliteration on B)
A waitlist page has no ad review, so the interval is safe. Test the heading against one other name and keep the winner as control.

**Small paid SaaS tool: a $19 per month tool that drafts replies to support emails.**
Inputs: buyer is founders who answer their own support inbox; outcome is an empty inbox each morning; monthly price.
- "Support Inbox Zero Autopilot" (Avatar, Goal, Container)
- "Clear Your Support Inbox in 15 Minutes" (Goal, Interval; not a quantified income or weight claim, so allowed on ad platforms)
- "Launch Week Founder Inbox Sprint" (Magnet, Avatar, Goal, Container)
Bonus naming: a bundled set of reply templates becomes "Angry Customer Reply Playbook" instead of "Template pack".

**Freelance service: website speed fixes for small online stores.**
- "Free 48 Hour Store Speed Audit" (Magnet, Interval, Goal, Container)
- "Shopify Owners Speed Sprint" (Avatar, Goal, Container)
Fatigue plan: when the name stops drawing replies after new screenshots and new copy have been tried, move the reason why to the next season ("Black Friday Speed Sprint") and keep the service and price the same.
