# Book examples for offer-market

Find the row that matches the user's situation and use its case as the illustration. Numbers are the book's.

## Cases by situation

| User's situation | Case to use | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Sales are weak although the offer and the salesperson are strong | Newspaper software: a product paid as a share of added ad revenue, sold to an industry shrinking about 25 percent a year | A shrinking market fails the growing test, and no offer work fixes that |
| The user thinks a better product or pitch is the main lever | Hot dog stand: the best advantage is a starving crowd, such as the only stand outside a stadium. Toilet paper in early 2020 sold for $100 or more a roll with no pitch | Demand in the market outranks offer and persuasion |
| Buyers have pain and are easy to find but rarely buy | Resume service for unemployed people | Fails purchasing power, so change the market |
| Ads or outreach reach people who do not convert | An offer built for wealthy doctors shown to nursing students | The audience reached must be the audience the offer was built for |
| Choosing between two candidate markets | Relationship coaching for people over 65 against coaching for college students | The first passes all four tests with more pain and more money |
| Defining the buyer and who to turn away | Microgym owners with about 100 members, a signed lease, at least one employee, and a goal of helping clients lose weight; personal trainers and online coaches turned away | A niche names concrete traits and names the excluded buyers |
| Worried the niche is too small | Single-niche companies past $30M a year exist for chiropractors, gyms, plumbers, solar installers, roofers and salon owners. About 1 in 250 businesses (0.4 percent) reach $10M a year | Nearly every business gains more from narrowing than from broadening |
| Setting a premium price against competitors | Competitors charged $500 a month, and one charged $5,000. The offer was priced at 3 times the highest and 32 times the lowest | A premium far above the range signals a different product |
| Doubts that price changes perceived value | Tasters rated the same wine higher at a higher stated price | Price alone raises the value the buyer experiences |
| Wants to cut the price to grow | The book claims 99 percent of businesses need to raise prices to grow | Raise value, then price, before any cut |
| Wants a before and after of a commodity offer turned premium | See the agency before-and-after case in the offer skill | That skill owns the case |

## Growth arithmetic

Use this when the user asks where growth comes from or needs lifetime gross profit for step 1. A business grows through more customers, a higher average purchase, or more purchases per customer.

- Revenue cap: 10 new clients a month at $1,000 lifetime value each caps revenue at $10,000 a month.
- Gross profit is price minus the direct cost of delivering one more unit. A $10 lotion that costs $2 to make has an $8 gross profit (80 percent). An agency service at $1,000 a month with $100 of labor has a $900 gross profit (90 percent). Net profit also subtracts rent, software and other overhead, so it is a different number.
- Lifetime gross profit: $1,000 a month at 90 percent gross margin, kept for 5 months, gives $4,500.
